How it works

Visual inspection. No cloud required.

A new product line has hundreds of good images, a handful of defects, and designs the company does not want in a vendor's cloud. NanoInspect learns it on the device and decides every part locally.

1Tier 1 · cheap LLMQwen2.5-VL-7B + LoRA fine-tuned on the Nano checks every part: verdict, defect type, location.
2Tier 2 · expensive LLMQwen3.8-27B compares doubtful parts with a known-good reference and explains what it sees.
3Tier 3 · human in the cloudOnly when review is cheaper than the risk. Only a crop is sent; labels return to retrain the edge.

Escalated cases

The escalation rule

escalate ⇔ min( P(defect | evidence) × $escaped defect , P(good | evidence) × $scrapped part ) > $human review

P(defect | evidence) comes from Bayes' rule using error rates measured on held-out parts and the line's defect rate. The costs are set under Quality rules → Escalation policy & costs.

Your quality procedure (SOP)

The SOP decides what happens to a defective part: reject or hold, the likely cause to check, and when to stop the line. Upload your plant's own SOP as a .json file; it is checked before it is used and the previous version is kept.

When does a part go to a human in the cloud?

      
After both edge models have looked at a part, it lands in one evidence bucket. We measured how often each bucket happens for defective and for good parts on held-out data. With your line's defect rate, Bayes' rule gives the probability that a part in that bucket is defective. A part is escalated only if the cheaper automatic decision still costs more than a human review. The quality manager sets these costs from the plant's cost-of-quality figures.
