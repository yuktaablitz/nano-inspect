Target user

Cost per part

Cloud AI and human inspection both scale with every part, even the obviously good ones.

The third path

Three tiers. Each is more expensive and sees fewer parts.

every part · verdict, defect type, location
Tier 2 · expensive LLMQwen3.8-27B + LoRA fine-tuned on the Nano, verified for DGX Spark
parts tier 1 is unsure about + 5% audit · compares with a known-good reference
Tier 3 · cloud humanonly when review is cheaper than the expected cost of a wrong automatic decision

Both LLMs are served by vLLM on the Nano. Only a small crop of an escalated part leaves the plant. The Nano keeps an outbox, so a network outage delays escalations but never stops the line. Human labels come back as training data.

Architecture
The escalation decision: explicit, defensible, measurable

Escalate only when a human review costs less than the risk.

escalate ⇔ min( P(defect | evidence) × $escape , P(good | evidence) × $scrap ) > $human review

P(defect | evidence) comes from Bayes' rule, using error rates measured on held-out parts and the line's own defect rate. The costs are the business's own numbers.

After the verdict: SOP-grounded machine instruction

A reject becomes an action the line can execute

  • SOP (ISO 9001 §8.7, versioned per product and defect type) decides the disposition, the containment and the process check.
  • Tier 2 writes the nonconformance report from what it sees, and may only pick a cause the SOP lists.
  • JSON, schema-validated, goes to the PLC / MES: divert to the reject or hold bin, or stop the line when a defect repeats.
  • The delta map (patch distance to 40 good parts) shows where the part differs, and crops what goes to the cloud.
